# Career Storytelling

## The goal

Make true work legible and compelling. The writing should reveal the context, complexity, judgment, contribution, and outcome that a bare task list hides.

## Story structure

1. **Context** — where and why the work happened.
2. **Tension** — what problem, constraint, or competing objective existed.
3. **Role** — what the user owned, decided, influenced, or supported.
4. **Action** — what the user actually did and why.
5. **Result** — what changed, with measured or qualified evidence.
6. **Capability** — what repeatable strength the story demonstrates.

## Resume bullet formula

`Action + object/problem + method or judgment + outcome/evidence`

Hypothetical example only:

Raw: "参与内部工单系统改造。"

Questions to resolve:
- Which workflow was broken?
- What did the person personally own?
- What analysis or design did they perform?
- Which stakeholders were involved?
- What changed after launch?

Possible rewrite only if confirmed:
"梳理客服与运营团队的工单流转问题，负责需求拆解和流程方案设计，协调研发及业务团队完成系统改造，减少跨团队重复流转。"

This example is not a factual claim about the user.

## Interview story bank

For each story, prepare:
- a 30-second summary;
- a 2-minute structured answer;
- deeper follow-up evidence;
- what the user personally contributed;
- what did not go as planned;
- lessons and transferability.

Use STAR or similar structures when helpful, but do not force every story into a formula if it obscures the real decision process.

## Language controls

- Replace generic adjectives with concrete actions.
- Avoid vague phrases such as "全面负责" unless scope is explicit.
- Do not overstate seniority or decision authority.
- Keep estimates visibly approximate.
- Prefer one strong, defensible claim over several weak claims.
- Tailor emphasis to the role without changing facts.
