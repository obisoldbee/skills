# Leader Methodology Adapted for Career Discovery

This reference adapts goal-clarification and execution-guardrail ideas into an interview contract.

## Interview contract

Before a substantial interview, establish:

1. **Purpose** — which career decision or output should this work support?
2. **Completion state** — what should the user know or possess after the work?
3. **Evidence** — what supports important claims?
4. **Integrity constraints** — which facts must not be inflated or disclosed?
5. **Scope** — which roles, periods, projects, and target jobs matter?
6. **Trade-offs** — breadth vs. depth, general profile vs. specific JD, speed vs. completeness.
7. **Unknowns** — which missing answers could change the final result?

Use the contract to guide question selection. Do not force the user to answer all seven at once.

## Decision register

Record material assumptions as:

| Decision | Current default | Status | Cost if wrong | User confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Target role | General product role | proposed | Medium | pending |
| Resume language | Chinese | proposed | Low | pending |
| Employer naming | Anonymize in working notes | proposed | Low | pending |
| Metric precision | Only user-confirmed figures | fixed rule | High | not applicable |

Defaults are provisional. Do not treat silence as consent for sensitive disclosure, external sharing, or irreversible actions.

## Stop and clarify

Pause the affected claim or output when:
- dates materially conflict;
- project ownership is unclear;
- a metric has no defensible source;
- a proposed title or seniority overstates the user's authority;
- confidentiality constraints are unclear;
- a user asks for a factual claim that contradicts supplied evidence.

Continue with unaffected parts and record the unresolved item.

## Evidence-based completion

A polished story is not complete merely because it reads well. It is complete when:
- the event is identifiable;
- the user's role is clear;
- the action and judgment are concrete;
- the outcome is appropriately qualified;
- the claim can survive a follow-up question.

A negative or incomplete outcome can still be a useful story if the user's decisions, learning, and contribution are clear.
